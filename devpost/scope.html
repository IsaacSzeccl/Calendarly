Calendarly

A standalone, calendar-aware poll for scheduling meetings with external parties. An AI reads the organizer's free-form constraints and explains the trade-offs between time slots.

The unique kernel

A hybrid poll that is calendar-aware. A guest with no account can just vote. A guest who connects Google Calendar gets matched on free/busy status. The organizer types constraints in plain language ("not Friday afternoons, Tom hates mornings, keep it under 90 minutes") and the AI turns them into ranking behavior and explains the trade-offs. Plain overlap is computed in code; the AI interprets and explains. The poll advises, and the organizer picks.

Standalone, with no Teams integration. Microsoft Teams' Scheduling Assistant is the main competitor and is strongest for internal scheduling. Calendarly targets external parties, where email ping-pong and Doodle polls are the pain.

The core loop

Poll advises, the organizer decides. Saved roster and "meet again" moved to Later.

Who it's for

A consultancy lead scheduling a workshop with about 6 people at a client company. Today they run email ping-pong or a Doodle poll. Internal meetings get one line of mention only; family and friends move to Later.

Inspiration & identity

Doodle for simple polls with no account, and Microsoft Teams' Scheduling Assistant as the competitor. The feel is extremely easy for beginners: guests respond from a link.

What "working" looks like

A demo video that tells the consultancy story, with a before-and-after: a real email ping-pong thread versus the same task in Calendarly.

  1. The organizer creates a poll and constraints, and shares the link.
  2. One guest votes without logging in; one guest connects a calendar.
  3. The "oh, that's cool" beat: the AI adds a slot and explains the trade-off.
  4. An earlier voter is notified by email.
  5. The organizer picks a slot, and the event and confirmation email go out.

Real Google test accounts are used wherever possible. The demo uses 4-5 accounts, with calendars designed to reflect complicated client agreements. Any seeded or fake accounts are labeled honestly.

Proof-of-concept boundary

Now In the proof of concept
  • Google Calendar only.
  • Organizer login, calendar connection, and poll creation with initial options from their free time.
  • Free-form constraints parsed by the AI; poll details can be entered in plain language and the AI helps fill them in.
  • Shareable link; guests need no account, give an email address, and can vote, add a time, or optionally connect Google Calendar.
  • Ranking with plain-language explanations that state their basis.
  • AI-added slots (at most 3 per poll) and the "new options added" email.
  • Organizer picks directly; event on their calendar; confirmation email with add-to-calendar link.
Later Worth doing, not now
  • Outlook and Apple calendars.
  • Groups of 50+, and monetization.
  • Native mobile apps.
  • Family-and-friends positioning.
  • Saved roster and "meet again" for repeat meetings.
Cut Left out, with reasons
  • Teams integration: you chose standalone so it can be monetized later.
  • Auto-lock voting: the poll advises, and the organizer picks directly.
  • AI-generated alternative meeting lengths: the AI may mention a different length but doesn't generate new-length slots.
  • Other calendar providers: each is its own OAuth setup and set of bugs.

Open items

  • Demo calendars: design the 4-5 demo accounts' calendars to reflect complicated client agreements.
  • Google verification: you will try to get the app verified. Review can take days to weeks and generally needs a public domain, a privacy policy, and a demo video, so it may not finish in time. Fallback: testing mode with listed test users.

Decided: roster and "meet again" go to Later; free-form constraints replace the earlier request form.
