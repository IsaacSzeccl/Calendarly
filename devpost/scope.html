Calendarly

An AI scheduling assistant for one organizer who needs to find a time with many other people. The organizer asks in plain language, and the AI ranks the best slots from everyone's real calendars and explains why.

The unique kernel

Ask the AI, don't poll the group. The organizer says what they need ("an hour with these people next week, afternoons"). The AI reads everyone's free/busy status and returns ranked slots with plain-language reasons, so nobody has to vote and check their calendar every time. After people have joined, "meet again" is nearly one click, because the roster is saved and slots are proposed automatically.

Standalone, not a Teams add-on. Microsoft Teams' Scheduling Assistant is the main competitor, and Calendarly aims for the same power without the manual clicking. For SMEs the pain point is Doodle polls: slow, and repeated for every meeting.

The core loop

They come back because the second meeting costs almost nothing. The people are already registered, and the AI already knows their availability.

Who it's for

Someone who organizes meetings with many other people, such as a consultancy lead booking a client workshop, or anyone opening a meeting or a talk. Today they email people back and forth, run a Doodle poll, or post a poll in a messaging app, then chase replies.

Inspiration & identity

Doodle for simplicity, and Microsoft Teams' Scheduling Assistant for power. The feel is extremely easy for beginners: nobody should get stuck or feel put off.

What "working" looks like

  1. The organizer asks the AI for a meeting.
  2. They send a link, and invitees log in and register.
  3. The AI auto-matches everyone's calendars.
  4. The organizer sees a ranking with reasons and picks a slot.
  5. The event is added to the real calendar automatically.
  6. The "oh, that's cool" beat: the organizer starts a new meeting with the same people, and the roster is already there with timeslots proposed.

Demo setup: one real Google account. The other 5–6 invitees are hosted fakes with seeded free/busy data, so ranking runs on realistic calendars and the event write-back shows live only on the real account.

Proof-of-concept boundary

Now In the proof of concept
  • Google Calendar only.
  • Plain-language request from the organizer to the AI.
  • Shareable invite link; invitees log in, register, and connect a calendar.
  • Free/busy matching and ranking with plain-language reasons, including slots where most people are free.
  • The organizer picks the slot directly, and the event is written to the real calendar.
  • Saved roster and "meet again" with automatic timeslot proposals.
  • A simple, beginner-friendly interface.
Later Worth doing, not now
  • Group voting as an alternative to the organizer picking.
  • Vote-only mode for people who don't connect a calendar (Doodle-style).
  • Microsoft Outlook and Apple calendars.
  • Email notifications.
  • Monetization, and groups of 50+.
Cut Left out, with reasons
  • Teams integration: you chose standalone so it can be monetized later.
  • Multiple calendar providers: each is its own OAuth setup and set of bugs, too much for the time available.
  • Voting and vote-only mode: the organizer picking directly is the recommended path and enough to prove the kernel.
  • Real calendars for the fake invitees: the demo uses seeded data.
